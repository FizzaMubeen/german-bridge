How to learn German for daily life

You do not need to study grammar for hours. Adults who live in a new country learn fastest with short daily practice, real sentences they need this week, and regular repetition. This app is built around that.

1. Little and often

10 to 15 minutes every day works better than two hours on Sunday. Your daily goal and streak are there to keep the habit, not to stress you.

2. Repeat just before you forget

A card you get right comes back after 1, 2, 4, 7, 15 and then 30 days. A card you miss comes back today. This is spaced repetition, and it is why "Today's plan" changes every day.

3. Think first, then reveal

Try to say the answer before you flip the card. The effort of remembering is what makes it stick, even when you get it wrong.

4. Sentences before grammar

Learn whole sentences for the places you go: the bakery, the U-Bahn, the Bürgeramt, the doctor, work. Grammar makes more sense once you already know a few examples.

5. Say it out loud

Tap Listen and repeat the German three times. Speaking it yourself trains your mouth and your ear, not only your eyes.

6. Always learn der, die, das

Learn the article with every noun. The colors help: blue der, red die, green das, purple for plurals.

7. Use your bridge language

Urdu and Hindi put verbs at the end, like German after weil and dass. Mujhe and mir work the same way. Use what you already know.

8. Make it about your life

Add your own words and sentences in My words: your job, your family, your street. Personal sentences are remembered far better than textbook ones.

Your 10-minute day: open Today, press Start, finish the cards due today, learn five new ones, add one sentence from your own day, and listen to each new card once. Once a week, print your "I want to learn" list as cut-out cards and keep them in your bag.

Gender: der · die · das A1

Urdu and Hindi have two genders, German has three, English has none. Gender does not carry over, so learn every noun with its article and its color.

der masculine
die feminine
das neuter, new for us
die every plural
Rules of thumb. Endings -ung, -heit, -keit, -schaft, -ion → die. Endings -chen, -lein, -um, -ment → das. Days, months, seasons and -er people (der Lehrer) → usually der.

Word order: where the verb goes A1

Urdu and Hindi put the verb at the end. English puts it after the subject. German keeps the conjugated verb in position 2 and sends any second verb to the end.

Verbs in the present tense A1

آپ / आप maps to Sie, تم / तुम maps to du. Use Sie with strangers, offices and at work until someone offers du.

Regular pattern (lernen): ich lerne, du lernst, er lernt, wir lernen, ihr lernt, sie/Sie lernen. Some verbs change the vowel for du and er: du isst, du fährst, er spricht.

Dativ: you already know it A2

Urdu and Hindi say "to me it is cold" (مجھے / मुझे). English flips it to "I am cold". German keeps our shape with mir.

Prepositions: before, not after A2

Urdu and Hindi put the small word after the noun (گھر میں / घर में). German puts it before, and it decides the case.

Wo? or Wohin? We use میں / में for both

in, an, auf, unter, über, vor, hinter, neben, zwischen take Dativ for location and Akkusativ for movement.

Connecting sentences A2

The linking word decides where the verb goes. After weil, dass, wenn and obwohl, German uses our word order.

Side by side

obwohl, trotzdem, deshalb B1

At B1 you join ideas. German has two families of linking words. obwohl, damit, bevor, nachdem, als, ob push the verb to the end, like Urdu and Hindi. trotzdem, deshalb, dann take slot 1, so the verb comes straight after them.

obwohl … verb at the end
trotzdem / deshalb + verb
Quick test: can you put a comma and start a new sentence? Then it is trotzdem or deshalb, and the verb comes next. If the clause can't stand alone, it is obwohl, and the verb waits until the end.

würde, hätte, wäre B1

Konjunktiv II is how German is polite and how it dreams: "would", "I wish", "if I were". You need just three words: würde + infinitive, hätte (would have), wäre (would be). Urdu and Hindi do the same with ہوتا / होता and گا / गा.
